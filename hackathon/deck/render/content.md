### Notes:
Haq is one agent that follows one case: a hospital stay. Before admission it turns the policy into a price for each room. During the stay it runs the regulator's clocks. After settlement it audits every line. If money is withheld, it drafts, sends with the family's OTP approval, and runs the grievance ladder in the order the regulator requires. Underneath all four, a human expert is one tap away and inherits the whole case.

### Notes:
The key design decision: the language model reads and writes, but it never produces a number. Every rupee comes from the entitlement engine, which is plain code with one rule per circular and a trace. The verifier blocks any message whose amounts do not match the engine or that lacks a citation. RAG is used only to quote the clause back to the user, never to decide. The orchestrator keeps one case per hospital stay, so the family and the human expert see the same history.

### Notes:
We designed the limits first. Haq never decides a claim; the insurer does. It never sends anything without the family's OTP. And it hands over to a human for medical necessity, fraud or non-disclosure allegations, big amounts, or low confidence. Two public lessons shaped this: Air Canada was held to what its chatbot promised, so every statement cites a clause; Klarna had to bring humans back for complex cases, so the human is one tap away from the start. Gartner found 64 percent of customers fear losing access to a human; we answer that directly.

### Notes:
Here is the arithmetic behind the demo. The bill is 2,27,500. The room was 8,000 a day against a 5,000 cap, so the insurer may apply a 0.625 proportion, but only to associated expenses like nursing, surgeon and OT fees. It applied it to everything. Haq recomputes: 62,325 of deductions are legitimate and Haq explains each one; 26,550 were cut from pharmacy, consumables and diagnostics, which IRDAI's 2020 circular exempts. ### Notes:
Everything in the stack is buildable in a hackathon, and every rail we mock has a real public spec: NHCX uses FHIR, DigiLocker and ABHA have sandboxes. The engine is generic: documents in, a versioned rulebook, clocks, and an escalation ladder. Our research found the same failure in lending and payments: RBI penalised all three credit bureaus in August 2026 for not paying the 100 rupees a day they owed, and failed UPI payments carry the same compensation. Each new journey needs a rulebook and tools, not a new product.
